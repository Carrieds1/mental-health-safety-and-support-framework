# Boundary Setting

Supporting people in distress requires clear and consistent boundaries.

Boundaries protect:

- the person receiving support
- the person providing support

---

## Core Idea

> **Support does not mean unlimited availability or responsibility.**

You can:

- be empathetic
- be supportive

Without:

- taking on responsibility for outcomes
- exceeding your role

---

## Why Boundaries Matter

Without boundaries:

- support becomes inconsistent
- burnout increases
- risk increases for both people

> Boundaries make support safer and more sustainable.

---

## Your Role

You are:

- Supportive
- Observant
- A pathway to help

You are not:

- A therapist
- Always available
- Responsible for fixing everything

> Support != responsibility for outcomes

---

## Understanding Distress-Driven Behaviour

When highly distressed, people may:

- become more urgent or demanding
- seek reassurance repeatedly
- struggle with perspective-taking
- push for exceptions or additional support

These behaviours are often:

> **driven by distress, not intention**

---

## When Support Becomes Unbalanced

Signs can include:

- repeated reassurance-seeking
- reliance on a single person
- increasing demands on time or attention

This may feel like:

- pressure
- guilt
- obligation

> Your role is to notice this - not absorb it.

---

## Signs Your Boundaries Are Slipping

You may notice:

### Thoughts

- feeling responsible for someone’s wellbeing
- guilt when saying no
- feeling like you are the only one helping

### Behaviours

- spending disproportionate time on one person
- responding outside expected times
- bending or breaking normal rules

> If it feels heavy, personal, or urgent - check your boundaries.

---

## How to Set Boundaries

### Principles

- Be clear and consistent
- Stay calm and neutral
- Focus on support pathways

### Example Responses

- “I’m here to support you, but I can’t stay after hours - let’s connect you with…”
- “I can’t make that exception, but I can help you find support”
- “That sounds really difficult - this might be best handled by…”

---

## Types of Boundaries

### Time Boundaries

- When are you available?
- When do you stop?
- Do you respond outside work hours?

---

### Role Boundaries

- What is your role?
- What is not your responsibility?
- When should you escalate instead of continuing?

---

### Emotional Boundaries

- You can care without taking it on
- You are not responsible for outcomes
- You do not need to “fix” the situation

---

### Capacity Boundaries

- How much time/energy can you give one person?
- Are you over-investing?
- Is the situation beyond your skill level?

---

### Consistency Boundaries

- Are you treating this person differently?
- Are you making exceptions you wouldn’t normally make?

---

## Professional Boundaries

### Keep interactions appropriate

- Maintain a professional role
- Avoid becoming a primary support person
- Do not blur roles (e.g. friend vs staff)

---

### Maintain appropriate communication

- Use official channels
- Keep tone professional
- Respond within expected timeframes

---

### Be aware of power differences

- You hold authority
- Avoid creating dependence
- Avoid exclusive or personal relationships

---

## When to Step Back

If someone:

- requires ongoing emotional support
- repeatedly returns only to you
- needs more than you can provide

Your role becomes:

> **Connect, don’t contain**

- Refer
- Escalate
- Involve others

---

## Practical Rule

> **Support within your role, skills, and capacity - escalate beyond it.**

---

## Final Reminder

> You do not need to absorb distress to provide meaningful support.

- Empathy without boundaries -> burnout
- Boundaries without empathy -> disengagement

> You need both
