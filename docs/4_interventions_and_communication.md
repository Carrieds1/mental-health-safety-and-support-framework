# Interventions and Communication

This document outlines practical strategies for communicating with and supporting someone in distress.

The goal is not to “fix” the situation, but to:

- reduce distress
- build rapport
- support safe engagement

---

## Core Idea

> **Relationship first. Interventions second.**

Interventions should only be used when they are:

- appropriate to the person’s state
- accepted (not resisted)
- supported by rapport

---

## Understanding Capacity (Window of Tolerance)

People move in and out of different states of emotional and cognitive capacity.

- When overwhelmed -> reduced ability to think, process, or respond
- When regulated -> better able to engage

### Practical Rule

- If someone is highly distressed -> focus on **relationship and safety**
- Avoid complex interventions until they are more regulated

> Do not confuse compliance with genuine capacity.

---

## Core Communication Approach

### Goal

- Understand the person
- Reduce pressure
- Maintain connection

---

## Active Listening (Foundation Skill)

Active listening involves:

- giving full attention
- allowing space to respond
- reflecting back what was said

### Key behaviours

- avoid interrupting
- use simple acknowledgements (“mm”, “okay”)
- summarise or restate

### Important nuance

- adjust for cultural differences
- respect different communication styles
- do not assume eye contact = engagement

---

## Key Communication Techniques

### Open-Ended Questions

Encourage expression and exploration:

- “How are you going with everything?”
- “What’s been most difficult?”

---

### Closed Questions

Useful when:

- gathering specific information
- reducing overwhelm

Example:

- “Are you safe right now?”

---

### Clarifying

Check your understanding:

- “Did I understand that correctly?”

---

### Empathy (Not Sympathy)

Demonstrate understanding without taking on the person’s emotions or situation.

- Reflect feelings
- Acknowledge the situation

Example:

> “That sounds really difficult.”

---

### Validation

Affirm their experience:

> “That makes sense given what you’re dealing with.”

---

### Summarising

- pull together key points
- confirm shared understanding

---

### Silence

Silence can be useful when:

- the person needs time
- emotions are present

Do not rush to fill silence.

---

## Asking Difficult Questions

When discussing sensitive topics:

### Use permission-based language

- “Can I ask you something about that?”
- “Is it okay if we talk about this?”

### Why this matters

- reduces pressure
- builds trust
- respects autonomy

---

## De-Escalation Principles

To reduce escalation:

- remain calm
- slow your pace
- use simple language
- avoid confrontation

### Non-verbal factors matter

- relaxed posture
- appropriate distance
- open body language
- consistent tone
- matching eye level (**important for reducing perceived power imbalance**)

> Non-verbal communication can influence safety, trust, and escalation more than words.

---

## Building Rapport

Rapport is built through:

- genuine interest
- consistency
- respectful communication

It may involve:

- small personal connection
- showing reliability
- following through

---

## Supporting Without Over-Directing

Avoid:

- jumping to solutions
- overloading with advice
- correcting too early

Instead:

- explore their perspective
- support their thinking
- guide gently

---

## Motivating Change (Basic Principles)

Focus on helping the person express:

- desire (“I want…”)
- ability (“I can…”)
- reasons (“this would help…”)
- need (“I have to…”)

### Simple method

1. Ask an open question
2. Listen
3. Ask a follow-up question

---

## Things to Avoid

### Communication traps

- interrupting or talking over
- arguing or correcting
- dismissing feelings
- rushing the interaction

---

### Questioning mistakes

- leading questions

  > “You’re calm now, aren’t you?”

- loaded questions
  > imply blame or expectation

---

### Behaviour to avoid

- becoming defensive
- showing impatience
- using authority to control
- trying to “logic away” emotions

---

### Over-intervening

> Not every situation needs an intervention.

If there is resistance:

- stop pushing
- return to relationship

---

## Practical Rule

> **If an intervention is resisted -> stop and refocus on the person.**

---

## Final Reminder

> You are not responsible for fixing the situation.

Your role is to:

- communicate effectively
- maintain safety
- support connection
