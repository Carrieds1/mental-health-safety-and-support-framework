# Core Principles for Supporting People in Distress

This document outlines practical principles for recognising and responding to distress in educational or support settings.

The focus is on:

- Safety
- Human connection
- Structured awareness
- Appropriate escalation

---

## Key Principle

> **Safety > Relationship > Intervention**

- Safety always comes first
- Relationship enables safe support
- Interventions only work once rapport is established

---

## 1. The Horse and Cart Model

When supporting someone in distress:

- The **horse** represents the relationship
- The **cart** represents interventions (questions, advice, actions)

### Key Idea

- Test interventions lightly
- If resistance occurs -> **return to relationship immediately**
- Do not push interventions without rapport

### Practical Examples

Interventions can include:

- Asking questions
- Suggesting actions (e.g. take a break)
- Redirecting focus
- Providing information

Start small. Larger interventions require stronger rapport.

---

## 2. Distress = Unmet Needs

Escalation is often a response to **unmet needs**, not intentional behaviour.

### Common Categories of Needs

- **Immediate:** interpersonal conflict, triggering events
- **Environmental:** noise, crowding, discomfort
- **External:** personal stressors (family, relationships)
- **Internal:** confusion, emotional or physical distress

### Key Idea

> **Behaviour is a response to need, not a problem to fix**

---

## 3. Threat Modelling (Risk-Aware Thinking)

When responding to a situation:

1. Consider possible risks
2. Prioritise by severity
3. Update understanding with new information

### Example

If someone reports chest pain:

- First consider life-threatening causes (such as heart attack)
- Then consider serious causes (lung caused)
- Then lower-risk explanations (gastric / anxiety)

> **Do not assume the cause - investigate by risk level**

---

## 4. Change from Baseline (Side Channels)

Behaviour should always be interpreted relative to a person’s **normal baseline**.

### What to Observe

- Engagement changes
- Communication changes
- Behavioural changes

### Key Idea

> **Behaviour is data, not a diagnosis**

---

## Practical Rules

- Notice **change**, not just behaviour
- Stay **curious**, not judgmental
- Avoid assumptions
- Adjust your approach based on response

---

## Escalation Rule

If there is potential risk:

> **Escalate when unsure - safety > certainty**

---

## Final Reminder

> People do not need perfect support - they need safe, human support.
